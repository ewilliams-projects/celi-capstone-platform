Targeted Analytics for Equitable Gas Decommissioning

As households that can afford it switch to electric heat and appliances, fewer gas customers are left to pay for maintaining and replacing aging gas pipes. The customers who remain are often renters and lower-income households, who already carry the highest energy burdens. This project is building tools to help cities and utilities find neighborhoods where planned, neighborhood-scale electrification can take the place of costly pipe replacement, so the people least able to afford the transition aren't left paying for a shrinking gas system. Where gas and electricity come from different utilities, the savings from retiring gas pipes don't automatically flow to the electric upgrades that replace them, so those areas will need different policies and mechanisms to make the change, and identifying them is just as important.

Known data limitations
  • Territories are approximate and are not official service territory maps. The utilities' own maps and the Colorado PUC's certificated territory maps are authoritative.
  • Some territories overlap in the source data. For example, the Town of Oak Creek's electric territory is drawn over roughly 2,300 square miles of Yampa Valley Electric's area, co-ops based in New Mexico and Oklahoma extend into southeast Colorado, and Xcel overlaps neighboring co-ops in places. Overlapping service areas that another utility also claims are shown as uncertain.
  • The Western Area Power Administration (WAPA) and Deseret Generation & Transmission are left out: they sell wholesale power to utilities, not to homes, but the source data draws them across most of the state.
  • City-owned utilities (Colorado Springs, Trinidad, Fort Morgan and Center) are never marked uncertain, because the source data draws co-op territories straight over towns.
  • Black Hills Colorado Electric and Black Hills Energy (gas) are separate companies with the same parent, Black Hills Corporation, so savings don't move between them as directly as within one utility.
  • The gas and electric data come from different sources and years, so their borders don't line up exactly.
  • Shapes are simplified to about 50 meters for faster loading and cut at a rectangle that approximates Colorado's border, so areas are approximate.
  • Customer counts in popups are for the whole utility, not for the area inside each shape.

Gas Planning Pilot Program communities

Under House Bill 24-1370, Xcel Energy, Colorado's only investor-owned utility that provides both gas and electricity to most of its customers, partners with local governments in its gas service territory to explore neighborhood-scale alternatives to replacing gas pipes. The Colorado PUC approved five pilot communities in June 2025 (Decision C25-0482): Boulder, Denver and Golden on the Front Range, and Winter Park and Breckenridge in the mountains. Xcel's June 2026 status report lists 16 potential projects: seven in Breckenridge, three in Winter Park, and two each in Denver, Golden and Boulder. Xcel and the communities must file their first projects, or explain why not, by June 1, 2027. Xcel provides both gas and electricity in four of the five; in Winter Park, Xcel provides gas but Mountain Parks Electric, a co-op, provides electricity.
